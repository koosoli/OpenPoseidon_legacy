# TW-WATER-03 — W3a: Tidewater shoreline waves

Work order: *OpenPoseidon × Tidewater — Water Plan v5*, package W3, first half.
Branch: `dec/tidewater-w3-shore`, on top of `dec/tidewater-w2-open-sea`.

W3 is split in two pushed steps so each stands on its own:

- **W3a (this note).** ShoreField + ShoreWaves: waves run in, shoal, plunge, become bores and run
  up the sand as a swash sheet with a per-pixel front.
- **W3b (next).** Breakers (lip sheets), ShoreSim (Eulerian foam, wetness, stranded foam), SurfFoam,
  and wet sand from the shore simulation.

## What is ported

These were ported source-faithfully from `dgreenheck/tidewater` @ `4811ba48` (MIT) and are credited
in `CREDITS.md`.

- **`ShoreField.js` → `water_tw/shore_field.rs`.** Fast Marching solution of |∇T| = 1/c with
  c = √(g·depth):
  - plane-wave sources on the border water cells;
  - the extension of the shoreline arrival time onto land (40 passes) and of T up the beach
    (24 passes, +h/1.5);
  - 3 smoothing passes;
  - direction × exposure.

  Constants are unchanged: maxDepth 25, minDepth 0.25, 4 m cells.
- **`ShoreWaves.js` → `water_tw/tw_shore.wgsl`.** The WGSL was generated from the source (`_code()`,
  the `normal` and `plain` evaluate variants plus the `world` one for W3b) and then renamed
  mechanically. It covers:
  - the travel-time phase, per-wave heights (sets, along-shore peaks, bar and rip channels) and
    Green's-law shoaling;
  - breaking at H > γ·d, the plunging tube, the bore and the roller relief;
  - the swash run-up with its lobed front;
  - crest translucency (`shoreCrestPath`) and the turbid surf-zone medium (`shoreSurfMedium`).
- **`WaterSurface.js` / `WaterMaterial.js`.** The shore paths of both are now in `tw_water.wgsl`
  (`T && SH`, without SIM / SF):
  - the smooth max onto the swash sheet (not on steep rock);
  - the reoriented detail normal on the tilted wave face;
  - shore foam and aeration;
  - the swash-edge clip with the backwash break-up, the bead, trail and meniscus;
  - `folded` from the shore normal;
  - the sky seen through thin crests (the one line W2 did not need).

## Adaptations to OP (also in the file headers)

- **One field for the whole map, not one per coast region.** Tidewater solves one 2 km island.
  OP solves the whole heightmap in one field: 4 m cells, coarsened only to stay within
  2048² (6.25 m on a 12.8 km map).
  - Consequence: there are no region seams. Plan §8's phase-continuity test holds by
    construction, and the second, rotated coast needs nothing special.
  - GPU memory is 12 bytes per cell: T and the shoreline T in rg32float, direction × exposure
    in rgba8snorm. That is 48 MB at 2048².
  - Plan §5 asked for per-region fields keyed by world and terrain hash. The single field
    replaces that. Note for the owner: this is the one structural deviation in W3a.
- **When it is computed.** On a worker thread, never the render thread, from the CPU heightmap
  the producer already uploads (`wgr_terrain_set_heightmap`).
  - It recomputes when the terrain is re-uploaded (a world load or terrain edit), when the sea
    level moves 0.25 m, or when the swell heading moves 5°.
  - The thresholds are measured from the field in use, not by rounding, so a value hovering at
    a boundary cannot start one solve after another. A solve that fails is not retried with
    the same inputs.
  - The previous field stays in use until the new one is ready. Shore waves are off until the
    first field exists.
  - There is no disk cache: the solve is expected to take seconds. Measuring that is in "Not
    verified" below.
- **Swell direction.** It is the heading of the FFT swell system (Water tab, TW swell heading;
  reference sea 4 turns it to 180°). Tidewater uses its own `WORLD.swellDir` for this.
- **Amplitude.** It follows the swell energy relative to Tidewater's default 0.48 (clamped
  0.25–2.5×), so OP's weather and reference seas move the surf. Tidewater has one sea state.
- **Terrain normal.** The run-up sheet is kept off steep rock using central differences of OP's
  heightmap. Tidewater bakes a terrain normal texture.
- **Along-shore coordinate.** It is measured across the swell axis from the field centre.
  Tidewater measures it across the local wave direction from the world origin, which stretches
  by |xz|/R wherever the fronts curve with radius R:
  - about 5× at the edge of Tidewater's island;
  - about 20× in a bay 6 km from OP's map centre, which would crush the ~100 m peaks and
    1.5 m swash lobes.

  Across the fixed axis it never compresses, and it stays continuous everywhere.
- **Arrival-time offset.** The FMM plane-wave start is offset by half the domain diagonal / c0
  + 1 s instead of Tidewater's `+ size` (12 800 s on a 12.8 km map), keeping f32 precision.
  A constant offset only shifts every wave alike.
- **Outside the map.** The heightmap repeats its edge heights there. The exposure fades to 0
  over one field cell, so no shore waves are drawn beyond the field.
- **`shoreDirAt` is not ported.** It is Tidewater's cheaper regional lookup. Its own fallback
  (`terrainShoreSample`) is used everywhere.
- **Wet sand is unchanged in W3a.** OP's own coast wet band keeps drawing. Plan W3 replaces it
  with the ShoreSim wet channel in Tidewater mode; that is W3b.

## A W2 correction: the sea now runs downwind

With Tidewater's evolution h0·e^{iωt} and the e^{+ik·x} inverse transform, the waves of a
spectrum peaked at angle *a* travel toward −(cos a, sin a). The angle is where they come *from*.

- OP's wind lane (`fft_wind_sea`) is the wind's velocity direction, i.e. where it blows *to*.
  W2 passed it unturned, so its sea ran upwind.
- The Water tab's swell heading is documented as where the swell travels to, and had the same
  problem.
- Both are now turned by 180° in `fft.rs` (test
  `directions_are_turned_so_the_sea_runs_downwind`).
- The shore field uses the same heading, so the shore waves arrive from where the FFT swell
  visibly comes from.
- **To check by eye:** waves should move with the flags and smoke, and the shore sets should
  arrive the way the open-sea swell runs.

## Water tab

The new controls are in the Tidewater group:

- **TW shore waves** switches the shore waves on or off.
- The Tidewater ShoreParams, all with Tidewater's defaults:
  - period 9 s, amplitude 0.34 m, variation 0.55;
  - breaker index 0.78, break span 0.13;
  - curl 1, run-up 1, turbidity 0.16.
- The profile saves them as a separate `tidewaterShore` line, so a W2 profile still reads.
- ABI: `WgrWaterParams::tidewater_shore[3]`. sizeof goes 1136 → 1184, and the layout hash
  refuses a stale pair.
