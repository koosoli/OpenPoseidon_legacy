# TW-WATER-08 — W3f: light under the water (Tidewater's water colour)

Branch: `dec/tidewater-w3f-colour`, on top of `dec/tidewater-w3e-boat-sea`.
Status: implemented. Visual check and owner acceptance pending.

## The report

Oli, 2026-09-27: W3 "looks good so far but colours are off and look nothing like the original
Tidewater or our previous implementation". Tidewater's deep water is a dark navy that turns
turquoise only over shallow sand (its README screenshots, Dec's Caribbean capture); ours was a
pale, even cyan over the whole bay, deep water included.

## Cause

The water material is a faithful port (absorption 0.42/0.075/0.035, scattering
0.012/0.018/0.024 1/m, the same in-scatter and Fresnel terms). What differs is the light on
whatever is seen through it. Tidewater installs `UnderwaterLighting` into every scene material:
the sun reaching a submerged point is attenuated along its refracted path, exp(-σt d / μ), and
the ambient over ~1.2 d (plus a little in-scattered blue). OP lights its sea bed as if it stood in
air, so every metre of sea showed sunlit sand through only the viewing path's blue filter: over
20 m of water that path alone leaves ~25 % of the blue and ~10 % of the green, which over a
full-bright sand reads as cyan; with the light's own ~23 m path down the green falls to ~1 % and
the blue to ~6 %, which is Tidewater's navy.

## Change

`water_tw/tw_water.wgsl`, above-water branch: the refracted scene sample (sea bed, hulls, piers
below the surface) is multiplied by Tidewater's two column attenuations, blended by the direct
share of a flat bed's irradiance (the opaque copy holds direct + ambient already summed). Depth is
the sample's own depth below the mean sea level, from the scene depth buffer.

Not ported: caustics and the wave-following light map (W5), and objects under water seen from
below the surface (the below-water branch samples the scene unmodified, as before).
