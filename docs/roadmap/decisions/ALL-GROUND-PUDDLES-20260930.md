# Universal ground puddles and liquid-rain ripples

The owner's September 30 clarification supersedes the earlier mud-only admission policy: puddles should form on ground on any map, including grass, soil, beaches and roads, grow with rain, and respond to rain hitting their surface. This is cosmetic standing water; it does not modify terrain, collision or vehicle/character geometry. Volumetric mud contact deformation remains a separate task and requires genuine soft-soil metadata.

## Source evidence and admission

The owner's installed WGPU Nogova session (`logs/cwr_2026-09-30_18-21-45_31700.log`) loaded `noe/noe.wrp` and reported **0 of 302** materials eligible under the superseded mud whitelist. Read-only retail `Noe.pbo`, `O.pbo` and base config inspection found Nogova terrain families such as `o/pole1.paa`, `pole2`, `blita2`, `b1`, `s2`, `pt`, `n1`, `l1`, and their four-quadrant composites. They resolve through the base Default fallback rather than the English mud/soil whitelist; the stock Everon `Field/files=pol/soundEnviron=dirt` exception cannot repair this map generally. This evidence does not prove that all brown textures are deformable mud.

Every actual terrain material now receives the universal-ground receiver flag. Language, surface-class fallback, mixed quadrants and image residency no longer veto puddles. Semantic classifiers remain unchanged for conservative soft-soil information: bit 0 is ground receiver, bit 1 is legacy/native soft soil, bits 8–13 describe authored soft-soil slots. Material size/flag offset remain 384/380 bytes. The source-weight denominator for authored soft soil remains independent of bound image weights. This deliberately changes the former grass/hard-ground negative contract; BeachGrass is now an eligible ground positive/control, not an expected dry negative.

Standing water retains near-level slope admission (`normal.y` .995–.9995), is removed by snow depth, and excludes submerged ground. The previous sea-level-plus-four-metre veto is replaced by a .15–.5 metre dry-land margin, allowing dry lowland and beaches. Raw zenith rain reach blocks covered ground. Its coverage-validity factor fails closed outside/without the directional map; ambient AO strength/floor do not supply rain proof. Turning off the entire physical map also suppresses puddles where cover is unknown. Far-map availability is a bounded limitation, not a full-world shelter guarantee.

## Shared shading and weather contract

`ground_puddles.wgsl` exports `ground_puddle_mask(world_xz, footprint, wetness)`, `ground_puddle_land_factor(normal_up, height, sea_level, snow_depth)` and `ground_puddle_ripple_normal(world_xz, time, rain, footprint)`. Callers provide absolute world coordinates and compute derivative footprint in uniform flow. Ground-road/object integration is separate and must use these same functions, raw zenith cover and coverage proof; this terrain commit alone does not establish road rendering acceptance.

The mask preserves the previously accepted world-fixed clustered field and wetness thresholds. Higher wetness lowers the threshold and expands connected film patches. It never animates with camera or time. Film fades with .4–.9 metre footprint; rain ripples fade sooner (.02–.09 metre), becoming **exactly** the flat mean normal when unresolved or rain is zero. Deterministic world-fixed drop cells emit radial wave packets with simulation-time phase, bounded slope (.028), spatial boundary fade and fade at both lifetime endpoints. Rain affects the surface normal inside the puddle film, not the terrain geometry. Reflection uses existing sky/sun shading; no extra reflection pass or texture is introduced.

The internal presentation snapshot captures whether effective rain particles are snowflakes. Terrain accumulation and ripple strength both use `UniformWettingDensity(weatherRain, effectiveParticles, snowflakes)`: mission rain remains authoritative, liquid particle overrides contribute, and falling snow alone contributes zero. Time remains the captured simulation clock, so pause freezes both accumulation and ripple phase. WgrTerrainParams still occupies 88 bytes: former `_pad1` at 72 holds wetness, former `_pad2` at 76 now holds effective **liquid** rain. Older producers send zero padding and therefore show no ripple; older consumers ignore the new rain lane. No public struct field count or size changes.

## Verification and acceptance limits

Standalone `scripts/Test-TerrainPuddles.ps1` passes the CPU receiver/soft-soil separation, liquid-rain versus snowflake accumulation, pause/reset and frame-rate checks. `git diff --check` passes. The new actual-helper GPU test contains 64 probes for film growth, dry/far exact removal, liquid-rain movement, bounded unit normals and slope/ocean/snow exclusions; the real terrain composition/source-contract tests are updated for the universal policy. These new Rust/GPU tests must be run by the root in its serial test window, followed by the combined game build and installed smoke. They were authored here but have not been run here.

Runtime acceptance still needs Nogova dry/rain/paused/drying controls, native grass and pavement/road controls, close moving-ripple versus far/aerial no-glitter controls, and the established floorless roof absent/present/removed fixture. Keep fixed-view controls, master-off equivalence, provenance and ownership rules. Neither changed source eligibility counts nor still screenshots alone prove appearance, temporal stability or road continuity. The earlier mud-only captures remain historical evidence for film placement and shelter behavior, not acceptance of this broader scope.
