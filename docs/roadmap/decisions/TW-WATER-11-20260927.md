# TW-WATER-11 — W3j: the water's glow (deep-water colour)

Branch: `dec/tidewater-w3j-glow`, on top of `dec/tidewater-w4a-spray`.
Status: implemented. Default value pending the owner's pick from the A/B captures.

## The report

Oli: the colours "look nothing like the original tidewater or our previous implementation".
Dec, after watching the colour runs replayed in the game: run 4 (Tidewater, reflection only) and
Current OP's water (runs 2 / 6) look best; asked for a mix of the two.

## Finding (W3h debug views)

* View 20, the surface's reflection (sky, SSR, sun glint): deep navy with a lighter sheen toward
  the sun. Matches Tidewater.
* View 21, what is seen through the water (sea bed, attenuated): dark over deep water, turquoise
  over sand. Matches Tidewater.
* View 22, light scattered inside the water toward the eye (Tidewater's single-scattering sun +
  ambient in-scatter and the crest subsurface term): pale cyan everywhere, as bright as the sky.
  This is what turned the whole sea cyan. The formula is Tidewater's verbatim; the light fed into it
  (OP's sun_diffuse x PI and sun_ambient) is several times stronger relative to OP's sky
  reflection than Tidewater's sun (~9) and sky (~1) are to its own sky. The W3i log line records
  the actual values for a later calibration of the units.

## Change

That in-scatter (and the same term under water) is multiplied by a new **TW water glow** scale
(Water tab; `WgrWaterParams::tidewater_fx.z`, profile key `tidewaterGlow`). 0 gives the reflection-
only look of view 20; 1 is the previous look. `WGR_TW_GLOW` overrides it per session for A/B
captures (0 / 0.06 / 0.12 / 0.25 were captured).

**Default 0** (W3j.1): Dec's pick from those captures, 2026-09-28 — the reflection-led navy of
run 4. From below the surface (the camera under water, looking up) the water's own colour is
kept at no less than 0.12 of Tidewater's, so the underside of the surface does not go black; the
underwater look is calibrated properly in W5.

W3f's seabed attenuation stays: it is what keeps the sand under deep water from showing through.
