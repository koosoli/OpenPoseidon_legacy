## Source diagnosis and bounded change

The installed log confirms the external near-blade photo atlas was uploaded: eight 64×256 layers. Read-only sampling of the installed `assets/grass/meadow-grass-blade-*.png` files shows olive and warm brown source colours, rather than neutral white. They are the actual external texture path, not the Rust generated-atlas fallback. The existing point and mean dry formulas both use a large luminance-independent straw floor of 0.55. Even a black input therefore gains bright straw in a dry patch; blue sky illumination can further mute its warm colour. This is a plausible contributor to the pale mass, not proof that it is the sole lighting/texture cause.

The candidate changes only the dry colour response: linear straw RGB `(0.74,0.66,0.32)` becomes `(0.70,0.58,0.20)`, floor 0.55 becomes 0.42, and source-luminance gain 1.35 becomes 1.25. Full straw's blue/red ratio drops from 0.432 to 0.286. Its luminance remains proportional to source brightness, with roughly 18–33% lower full-straw luminance over nonnegative inputs; partially dry patches change less. It is a warm reflectance adjustment, independent of the new sun sheen.

One shared pure WGSL palette feeds both actual point shading and `blade_dry_mean`. The exact affine integral retains the correlation between source colour, height-weighted colour and dry-tip bias. Patch amount/scale, deterministic world hash, root/tip mask, geometry, placement, wind, coverage/alpha, diffuse normals, width/height filtering and broad sun response remain unchanged. Patch zero is exactly inert. Native authored blade colour retains its existing explicit bypass of the procedural dry/grade chain.
