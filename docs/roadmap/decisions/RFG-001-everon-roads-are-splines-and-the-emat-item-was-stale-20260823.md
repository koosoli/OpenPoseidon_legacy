# RFG-001 — Everon's streets are generated splines, and the `.emat` item was stale

**Date:** 2026-08-23
**Branch:** `feat/consolidated`
**Status:** MEASURED. Roads are NOT implemented; this records that they are
reconstructible and exactly what from. The `.emat` finding is a correction to
[HANDOVER-20260823](../HANDOVER-20260823-reforger-finish.md), which overstated it
by two orders of magnitude.

## The street is missing because we import placements, not splines

Owner report: at Everon `4743.55 / 3990.93` there should be a street and there is
none. Within 400 m of that point `ev_DALL.wrp` holds:

| | |
|---|---|
| `structures\infrastructure\pavements` placements | **262** |
| `props\infrastructure\roadblock_01` | 2 |
| road **surface** models | **0** |
| road models anywhere in the 1,123-model table | **0** |

Kerbs and footpaths are prefabs, so they import. The surface between them is
generated by Enfusion from a spline at world-build time and exists as no model at
all, so `xob export` — which converts prefab placements — has nothing to carry
across. The result is the outline of every street with grass down the middle,
which is what the owner is looking at.

## The splines ARE in the world file, and the hierarchy is this

Confirmed by walking `worlds/Eden/Eden.ent` (70 MB) with a reader that reproduces
the C++ `EbinWorld` count exactly (1,230,897 placements):

```
RoadGeneratorEntity   2,207   depth 1, real world coords
                              RoadWidth, RoadFalloffWidth, FalloffStartWidth,
                              AdjustHeightMap, Flags
  └─ RoadEntity       2,200   child blocks; Width (1.75 .. 40.0 m, median 10.0),
                              IsClosedSpline
       └─ ShapePoint          arrays of local Position
```

Spline points recoverable today: **2,491** — RoadEntity 1,021, SplineShapeEntity
1,343, PolylineShapeEntity 127.

Two traps found while measuring, both of which make the data look absent:

1. **Spline points live inside ARRAYS of entity blocks.** A walker that does not
   pass the current entity down into array elements recovers 3,222 of them
   instead of the real count and reports a mean of 1.35 points per road against a
   maximum of 32. It looks like sparse data; it is a walker bug.
2. **A `RoadEntity`'s own `coords` are near zero and its points are local.** The
   world position comes from the parent `RoadGeneratorEntity`. Reading the child
   alone puts every road at the map origin — which is easy to mistake for "the
   road data is corrupt" rather than "you did not compose the transform".

Also checked, so nobody repeats it: there is no second Eden layer to read. The
corpus holds exactly two `.ent` for this world, `Eden.ent` and `EmptyEden.ent`
(9,795 bytes).

## What building it takes

1. The EBIN reader is flat — `EbinPlacement` keeps a transform and discards the
   hierarchy, and drops entities without `coords`. It needs to retain the
   generator -> road -> point chain, or gain a targeted road extractor.
2. Tessellate each spline to a ribbon at `RoadWidth`, with the
   `FalloffStartWidth` / `RoadFalloffWidth` shoulder, conformed to terrain.
3. Emit as either a generated `.p3d` per spline plus placements (reuses the whole
   existing model+manifest path) or as real OPRW road-network links (the block is
   currently written as one zero per land cell, and the engine has a road path —
   `ROAD_GPUPATH` appears in every capture log).
4. A surface material. This is why the item is sequenced after `.emat` below.

## Correction: the `.emat` item was measured wrong

The handover records **"1,275 of 1,299 materials (98.2%) resolve with no albedo,
no normal map, no specular map and an empty shader family"**, attributed to
`TranslateEmat` reading DayZ's `AlbedoMap` / `NormalMap` while Reforger writes
`BCRMap` / `NMOMap` / `MaskMap`.

`EmatMaterialAdapter.hpp:322-324` already binds `BCRMap`, `NMOMap`,
`GlobalNMOMap`, `NTCMap` and `MaskMap`. Measured over one Reforger frame on the
current build, 2,475 materials touched:

| | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| no albedo | **30** | 1.2% |
| empty shader family | 122 | 4.9% |
| no normal map | 721 | 29.1% |
| no specular map | 1,184 | 47.8% |

Families resolve: MatPBRMulti 1,104, MatPBRBasic 758, MatPBRTreeCrown 194,
MatPBRTreeTrunk 88, MatPBRDecal 68.

So the headline number is stale by roughly two orders of magnitude — it describes
a state that predates the `BCRMap` work. The real remaining gap is narrower and
differently shaped: normal and specular maps on a large minority of materials.
That is worth doing and is not "Everon does not look right"; it is a polish pass.

**Do not re-plan against the 98.2% figure.**
