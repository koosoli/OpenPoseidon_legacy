# TW-WATER-04 — W3b: Tidewater shore simulation and wet sand

Work order: *OpenPoseidon × Tidewater — Water Plan v5*, package W3, second step.
Branch: `dec/tidewater-w3-shore`, after W3a (TW-WATER-03).

## What is ported

These were ported source-faithfully from `dgreenheck/tidewater` @ `4811ba48` (MIT) and are credited
in `CREDITS.md`.

- **`ShoreSim.js` → `water_tw/shore_sim.rs`, `tw_sim.wgsl`, `tw_sim_kernel.wgsl`.** The Eulerian
  surf/swash state, one texel per ~0.5 m:
  - foam carried by the water: made by the roller, the plunge point and the swash front;
    semi-Lagrangian advection; thinned where the flow spreads it;
  - sand wetness, which dries over 28 s;
  - foam stranded when the water drains, which pops over ~5 s;
  - the flow speed.

  The kernel and the material readers are Tidewater's line for line.
- **`SurfFoam.js` `makeLaceTexture` → `water_tw/lace.rs`.** The 512² lace (strand distance,
  bubbles, mottling, cell id) and its CPU mip chain. It is bit-exact with the JavaScript: its
  FNV hashes are pinned in its tests, and it was checked byte for byte against node.
- **The material's SIM path (`WaterSurface.js` / `WaterMaterial.js`).**
  - The foam the water carries goes into the foam sum, with the clear plunging face kept free
    of it.
  - The shore waves' own foam is weighted 0.55 under the simulation.
  - Sediment clouds (`sandK`) follow where the bores left foam.
- **Wet sand on OP's terrain (App.js `terrainWetness`, Terrain.js).**
  - Tidewater mode reads wetness from the simulation's wet channel. Outside the simulated
    regions Tidewater's static damp band applies.
  - Wet sand is darker (wetDarken 0.58), slightly more saturated and cooler.
  - Stranded foam draws as the lace lines and bubbles of `shoreSimSandFoam`.
  - OP's own coast wet band is off in Tidewater mode, as plan W3 asks.

## Adaptations to OP (also in the file headers)

- **Two regions, not one.**
  - Tidewater simulates one fixed 380 m, 768² region over its beach. OP keeps up to two such
    regions (plan: K = 2) as the two layers of one texture array, on the coast nearest the
    camera.
  - The coast is indexed when the shore field is computed: 190 m blocks with at least 12
    surf-zone cells (0.1–2 m deep).
  - A region is kept until a candidate is 150 m nearer. Two regions are at least 300 m apart.
    Coast farther than 1.2 km is not simulated.
  - A point reads the region it is deepest inside.
- **Warm-up.** A new region starts empty and fades in over 3 s of simulation, so a half-built
  state never pops in. A region the camera leaves is dropped and its layer reused.
- **Beyond the regions** the shore waves keep their analytic foam. The material blends the SIM
  and non-SIM weights by the region's coverage; Tidewater decides this at compile time for its
  one region.
- **Heights** are taken relative to OP's sea level. Tidewater's sea level is 0, so its
  `ground > 3.2` becomes `ground - seaLevel > 3.2` here.
- **Spray deposits** (`shoreSimDepositAt`) arrive with the spray in W4. Until then no drops fall
  back into the water.
- **Terrain hookup.** OP's terrain gets one bind group more (group 3, `terrain/shore_wet.rs`).
  With Current OP it is a placeholder, and the shader keeps OP's wet band byte for byte.
- **GPU timer.** New row 80, "Tidewater: shore sim".

## Water tab

- **TW shore simulation** switches it on or off.
- **TW sand drying time** defaults to 28 s.
- Both are saved as the profile line `tidewaterSim`.
- ABI: `tidewater_shore[2].y/z`. These lanes existed but were reserved, so the struct size is
  unchanged.
