# TW-WATER-10 — W4a: breaker spray (Spray.js + the Breakers emitters)

Branch: `dec/tidewater-w4a-spray`, on top of `dec/tidewater-w3i-light-log`.
Status: implemented. Rust tests / build on the PC, visual check and owner acceptance pending.

## What Tidewater does

`src/fx/Spray.js` keeps one GPU ring of particles (drops, ligaments, dense spray, mist, clear
sheets). `src/ocean/Breakers.js` emits into it from the same compute kernel that finds the breaker
crests, at rates that follow the energy the breaker releases, in six stages of the break: drops and
torn strands off the falling lip, the splash-up where it lands (a fast burst of drops, strands and
dense torn sheets, with mist that lingers), the puff blown out of the collapsing barrel, the
roller's spray, the bore running into the backwash, and spindrift blown back off the crest in an
offshore wind. An update kernel integrates them (gravity, drag toward the wind, turbulence; drops
die when they fall back in) and a sprite material draws them: clear drops as motion-blurred
streaks whose opacity conserves their cross-section, dense spray white from every side with a
forward lobe, mist as a thin forward-scattering veil, all shadowed by the wave that made them.

## Port

| Tidewater | OP |
|---|---|
| Spray.js sprayCommon / emitter API | `water_tw/tw_spray_common.wgsl`, `tw_spray_emit.wgsl` |
| Breakers.js `_emitCode` | `tw_spray_emit.wgsl` (`breakersEmit`, called from `breakersProcessCrest`) |
| Spray.js `_buildUpdate` | `tw_spray_update.wgsl` (`spray_update`, second entry of the breaker module) |
| Spray.js `_buildMesh` material, Breakers `breakersSprayShadow` | `tw_spray.wgsl` (`vs_spray` / `fs_spray` in the water module) |
| Spray.js ring, textures | `water_tw/spray.rs` |

Constants, rates, sizes, lifetimes, per-kind drag / gravity / alpha and the lighting are
Tidewater's (dgreenheck/tidewater @ 4811ba48, MIT).

## OP differences

* **GPU emitters only.** Tidewater's CPU-owned tail of the ring (its boat's bow sheet and wake
  splashes) and its collision body are not ported: OP's vessels are not wired to the spray yet
  (W4b).
* **Budget on the GPU.** Tidewater reads the ring head back a few frames late and eases the
  emission scale so the ring holds about 2 s of spray. Here the update kernel's first thread does
  the same step every frame from the head it can read directly (no readback, no CPU state).
* **One ring buffer, one sprite texture.** The water's bind group is already past 16 sampled
  textures per stage (the camera group carries 8), so the three particle arrays share one storage
  buffer and Tidewater's two sprite textures (puff noise, drops) share one rgba8 image (the puff
  noise is generated at 128 instead of 64: the same tileable function, sampled finer).
* **Water height for the particles** is sea level plus the FFT's and the shore waves' vertical
  displacement at the particle, rather than Tidewater's full water query; it only decides when a
  drop has fallen back in.
* **No foam deposit** into the shore simulation when drops fall back in (Tidewater accumulates
  those in a storage buffer its ShoreSim reads; OP's shore state is written by its own kernel).
* **Light** in Tidewater's units from OP's sun and ambient, as for the lip (W3d); OP's fog.
* **Where.** Spray exists where the breakers do: inside the two shore-simulation regions near the
  camera, within 260 m of it (Tidewater `emitRange`), drawn to 320 m (`maxDistance`).

## Controls

Water tab (Tidewater): **TW breaker spray** (emission gain, default 1, 0 = off) and **TW spray
intensity** (sprite opacity, default 1). They travel in a new lane, `WgrWaterParams::tidewater_fx`
(sizeof 1200 -> 1216 with the Rust side), and are saved in the water-look profile
(`tidewaterSpray`).

## Also on this branch's base (W3i)

A log line every ~600 frames while the Tidewater backend renders: the sun and ambient light and
fog colour the water is lit with, so the water colour can be calibrated against Tidewater's
units (its sun irradiance is ~9 at noon, its sky irradiance / PI ~1, exposure 0.55).
